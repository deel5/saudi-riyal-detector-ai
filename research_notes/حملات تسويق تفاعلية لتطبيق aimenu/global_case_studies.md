# Global Case Studies: Interactive "Investigate-the-Clues" Marketing (Lost Phone, ARGs, Puzzle Hunts, Fake Profiles, Chat-Driven Narratives)

Research date: 2026-11-17. Notes on method: web search worked, but direct page fetches were blocked in this session (Wikipedia, Fast Company, mcdonalds.ca, greatdeals.com.sg and mssngpeces.com would not resolve). So every finding below comes from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Most results figures are self-reported in agency award entries (Cannes/Effie/D&AD) and have not been independently audited.

## Q1. Which "lost phone" / found-device campaigns exist, and how were they built?

### Takeaway
The documented "lost phone" examples split into two groups. (a) Commercial games built entirely around a fake phone UI (A Normal Lost Phone, Sara Is Missing, Simulacra 1-3) prove that browsing photos, chats and apps to rebuild a story is a format people will pay for and play for hours. (b) Brand campaigns that use a real phone or the phone channel itself: Global TV's seeded phones, Netflix's AirDrop texts for *Mr. Harrigan's Phone*, Doritos' "Phone Fright" calls in Hotel 626, and the Ex Machina Tinder bot. I found no well-documented food or delivery brand that has run a pure "browse a stranger's lost phone" web app. That looks like an open gap.

### Cited Findings
**Case 1: A Normal Lost Phone (Accidental Queens, France), 2017. Reference game for the format, not an ad.**
- Mechanic: "you have to search the phone's text messages, pictures and applications, in order to learn about its former owner." The player investigates the phone of Sam, who lives in the fictional city of Melren. — [anormallostphone.com](https://anormallostphone.com/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/A_Normal_Lost_Phone)
- Build: the text messages, calendar app and music player are all made to look and feel like a real smartphone. Released on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS and Nintendo Switch (Jan 26, 2017). — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017/01/27/a-normal-lost-phone-game-launch/); [AppUnwrapper](https://www.appunwrapper.com/2017/01/17/a-normal-lost-phone-releasing-january-26th/)
- Development path: it started as a free browser game-jam prototype and then got funding to grow into a full game with more puzzles, apps and story. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/from-game-jam-to-indie-hit-the-making-of-a-normal-lost-phone)

**Case 2: Sara Is Missing and Simulacra 1/2/3 (Kaigan Games, Malaysia), 2016-2022.**
- Simulacra (Oct 26, 2017) is a "found phone" horror game: a branching story with five endings and more than 5 hours of play. Kaigan says mobile is the best way to play it. It is the spiritual sequel to *Sara Is Missing*. — [Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/videos/found-phone-horror-game-simulacra-arrives-on-pc-mobile/277767/); [Gamepressure](https://gamepressure.com/games/simulacra/zf5532)
- Simulacra 2 is a detective thriller about the death of a popular influencer. Simulacra 3 came out Oct 25, 2022. — [PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/simulacra-2/simulacra-2-kaigan-games-creepy-detective-thriller-launches-for-ios-and-android); [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/807312/simulacra-3-review-kaigan-games-malaysia-horror)

**Case 3: Global TV "Global Touch" (Canada), for the Fox drama *Touch*, about 2012.**
- 175 real phones were left in Canadian cities, each wrapped with instructions to pass it on. Finders photographed something that inspired them and texted it to a preset number. The photos fed an online gallery, and each photo triggered a donation to Make-A-Wish Canada. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/1680279/marketing-through-lost-phones-the-global-touch-campaign)

**Case 4: Netflix *Mr. Harrigan's Phone* (agency David Miami), Oct 2022.**
- Billboards in Times Square and on Sunset Blvd showed cryptic text-message bubbles. Passersby received AirDrop requests carrying the billboard image, labelled as sent from "Sleepy Hollow Cemetery", the film's fictional burial place for the phone. A phone-burial stunt was also run. — [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2022/10/17/promotion-tactics-for-netflix-s-adaptation-of-stephen-king-s-mr-harrigan-s-phone-include-airdropping-thousands-of-passerby)

**Case 5: Doritos "Hotel 626" (Goodby Silverstein & Partners), Halloween 2008. Food brand.**
- An interactive horror website open only from 6pm to 6am EST, with 13 rooms of scary challenges. Visitors could opt in to their webcam. In "Phone Fright", phone directions reacted in real time to the action on screen. — [Cannes Lions entry](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/doritos-290056); [ABC News / Frito-Lay statement](https://abcnews.go.com/WN/statement-frito-lay-company/story?id=8969970); [AdForum "Phone Fright"](https://www.adforum.com/talent/49922-marc-sobier/work/12659558)
- Claimed results: over 4 million unique visitors with zero traditional media spend, 13-minute average stay, 136 countries (agency entries). — [Cannes Lions](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/doritos-290056); [Cannes Titanium entry](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/entries/snack-strong-productions-291354)
- Sales: over 2 million bags of the revived flavors were sold, selling out in 3 weeks. This figure comes from an exhibit attached to a complaint against Frito-Lay, so treat it with caution. — [CDD complaint appendix (PDF)](https://www.foodpolitics.com/wp-content/uploads/Final-Appendices-to-CDD-Complaint.pdf)
- Awards: D&AD Yellow Pencil and Wood Pencil (2009). A sequel, "Asylum 626", followed in 2009-10. — [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/work/d-ad-awards-archive/hotel-626)

**Case 6: *Ex Machina* Tinder bot "Ava" (A24), SXSW March 2015. Fake social profile plus chatbot.**
- SXSW Tinder users matched with "Ava, 25", whose photos were of the actress Alicia Vikander. After a text chat the bot sent users to the Instagram account @MeetAva, which revealed the film promo. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1132355); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2015/03/16/ex-machina-tinder-heartbreaker/); [Time](https://time.com/3746083/tinder-sxsw-catfishing-ex-machina)
- Claimed result: per Amobee data cited by ClickZ, *Ex Machina* consumption rose 353% over March 14-15 against the previous two days. Coverage split between "brilliant" and "deceptive". — [ClickZ](https://clickz.com/?p=26814); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/tinder-marketing-stunt-at-sxsw-brillant-or-deceptive/375188/)

### Inferences
- The technical pattern for a lost-phone experience is well proven: a mobile-first web or app shell that mimics a phone's lock screen, Messages, Gallery, Notes, Calendar and Music. Story clues are spread across the apps, and passcodes for locked apps are hidden in other apps (as in A Normal Lost Phone). A brand could build this as a web app with no download needed.
- Brand versions get stronger when the fiction leaks into real channels: a real phone number to text, a real Instagram account, AirDrop, phone calls. Ex Machina, Mr. Harrigan and Hotel 626 all did this.

### Gaps
- I found no verifiable Burger King, McDonald's, KFC, Domino's, Uber Eats, Deliveroo, Talabat or Careem campaign built specifically on the "lost phone" format.
- *Replica* (SOMI, 2016) returned no usable results in my searches, so it is not documented here.
- No sales or player figures were found for Simulacra or A Normal Lost Phone.
- The exact year of Global Touch is unconfirmed because the page could not be fetched. About 2012 is an inference from *Touch*'s air dates.

## Q2. What mechanics drove participation (rewards, hidden promo codes, leaderboards, UGC, community solving)?

### Takeaway
The biggest results come from multi-channel ARGs where communities solve together (Why So Serious: 10-11M participants). The most transferable mechanics for food and app brands are: rewards for being first (Jay-Z Decoded), promo codes hidden across a brand's own ads and social posts (McDonald's), branded in-world games (KFC, Baskin-Robbins), and messaging-app delivery (Hellmann's on WhatsApp, Eva.Stories on Instagram).

### Cited Findings
**Case 7: Warner Bros. *The Dark Knight* "Why So Serious?" (42 Entertainment), 2007-08.**
- A 360-degree alternate reality experience that ran for about 15 months before the film's release (Guinness says 14). Players dressed as the Joker and joined a fictional election campaign for Harvey Dent. — [42 Entertainment](https://42entertainment.com/work/whysoserious); [Guinness World Records](https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/98707-most-popular-alternate-reality-game-arg)
- Results: more than 10M unique active participants in over 75 countries (award entry); the agency says over 11M. Guinness record for "most popular ARG". Cyber Lions Grand Prix (Viral) at Cannes 2009 and a 2009 Webby. — [Cannes Lions entry](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/entries/why-so-serious-296907); [Gizmodo interview](https://gizmodo.com/dark-knight-arg-interview-immersive-gaming-batman-1850782310); [ClickZ](https://clickz.com/?p=67833); [Webby](https://winners.webbyawards.com/2009/advertising-media-pr/individual/viral-marketing/151677/why-so-serious-the-dark-knight-alternate-reality-game)

**Case 8: McDonald's "The Lost Ring" (AKQA with Jane McGonigal), Mar-Aug 2008, for the Beijing Olympics. QSR brand.**
- A global ARG where players pieced together historical documents, secret codes and an academic character. It ran from about Feb 29 or Mar 3 to Aug 24, 2008 (sources differ). — [The Lost Ring (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Ring); [Campaign Asia](https://www.campaignasia.com/article/the-ioc-the-lost-ring/8nvbgfuk3499u1a2bte0wen1n5)
- Results: more than 2 million users took part (MediaPost). One Show recognition in New Media Innovation (tier not stated). One secondary case summary cites the AdweekMedia BUZZ3 Grand Prize (unverified). — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/89387/akqa-mcdonalds.html); [The One Show](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/theoneshow/-client/McDonald's/2009); [ICMR case](https://icmrindia.org/casestudies/catalogue/Marketing/MKTG215.htm)
- Notably light branding: McDonald's sponsored the game without heavy product placement (my reading of the ICMR case title and focus; the full text was not read).

**Case 9: Bing x Jay-Z "Decoded" (Droga5), Oct-Nov 2010. Real-world plus digital puzzle hunt.**
- All 300+ pages of Jay-Z's memoir were hidden in real places (billboards, objects, buildings) around the world. Players solved clues in Bing Maps, and the first to find a page and check in could win prizes. The grand prize was described as lifetime Jay-Z concert tickets, while Microsoft's release says New Year's Eve tickets in Las Vegas. — [Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2010/10/17/jay-z-and-bing-change-the-game-with-decoded/); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/adfreak/droga5-bing-play-location-game-jay-z-12053)
- Awards: Cannes 2011 Outdoor Grand Prix and Integrated Grand Prix, plus 3 Gold, 1 Silver and 1 Bronze. Whether it also won a Titanium Lion is disputed between sources. — [Cannes Lions](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/decode-jay-z-with-bing-280392); [Adland](https://www.adland.tv/cannes-integrated-grand-prix-and-titanium-2011); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/1679208/cannes-anatomy-of-jay-zbing-decoded)

**Case 10: McDonald's hidden promo-code hunts. QSR; the closest match to "promo codes hidden in clues".**
- McDonald's Singapore "McCrispy Crunch" (Apr 2026): fans looked for hidden codes across social media and online ads and redeemed them in the McDonald's app for discounts, one being 50% off a McCrispy meal. Three of five codes had been found at the time of reporting. — [GreatDeals SG](https://www.greatdeals.com.sg/2026/04/21/mcdonalds-hidden-mccrispy-crunch-promo-codes-3-found)
- McDonald's Canada "EAT_THE_CODE": a campaign page where players hunt for a hidden promo code (details and date unconfirmed; page not fetchable). — [McDonald's Canada](https://www4.mcdonalds.ca/eatthecode/)

**Case 11: Baskin-Robbins x Netflix "Scoops Ahoy / Operation Scoop Snoop" (22squared and MSSNG PECES), summer 2019. Food brand plus a fictional in-show brand.**
- Baskin-Robbins brought *Stranger Things 3*'s fictional ice cream shop, Scoops Ahoy, to life and built an AR spy game, "Operation Scoop Snoop", using only "1985 technology" to take players back to 1985. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/retail/baskin-robbins-and-netflix-made-a-spy-game-for-stranger-things-that-uses-only-1985-tech/); [Effie](https://effie.org/case_studies/case/US_2020_E-4772-170)
- Claimed results: over 500,000 players, 57 years of total gameplay, and about 4 billion impressions (200M paid, 3.7B earned) over the 2-month partnership. Cannes Lions entry, Effie case. — [Cannes Lions](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/scoops-ahoy-operation-scoop-snoop-724663)
- Baskin-Robbins Australia ran a separate Stranger Things/Instagram effort. It reported market share among 13-24 year olds rising from 29% to 45% (not the AR game). — [Franchise Executives](https://franchiseexecutives.com.au/stranger-things-campaign-success-baskin-robbins/)

**Case 12: Netflix *Stranger Things 4* puzzle trail with Candy Digital, 2022.**
- Clues were hidden in scenes on a website, in Netflix's own marketing content and in the show itself. Solving them involved a browser-based Lite-Brite. Reddit fans found the countdown site, which turned out to be an NFT/collectibles drop, and reacted badly. That counts as a partial failure. — [Agency creative-lead portfolio](https://ayzcg.squarespace.com/strangerthings); [What's on Netflix](https://whats-on-netflix.com/news/stranger-things-nfts-to-launch-ahead-of-season-4)
- Season 5 (2025): daily puzzles in the Netflix Puzzled app until the finale on New Year's Eve, plus cryptic posters for fans to decode. — [The Streamable](https://thestreamable.com/netflix-puzzled-stranger-things-theme-season-5)

**Case 13: KFC "I Love You, Colonel Sanders! A Finger Lickin' Good Dating Simulator" (Psyop), Sept 24, 2019. QSR branded narrative game.**
- A free visual-novel game on Steam. Peak of 4,372 concurrent players (Sept 25, 2019). About 12,500-12,700 reviews rated "Very Positive" (snapshots from different dates). — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1121910/I); [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/1121910/charts); [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/kfc-launches-i-love-you-colonel-sanders-dating-simulator/)

**Case 14: Hellmann's "WhatsCook" (CUBOCC, São Paulo / Unilever), 2014-15. WhatsApp-driven food brand experience.**
- Users entered their phone number on a website. Real chefs then contacted them on WhatsApp and taught them to cook a dish with the ingredients they had at home, plus Hellmann's. Brazil ran first for two weeks, followed by Argentina, Chile, Paraguay and Uruguay for 3 weeks each. — [Cannes Lions entry](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/hellmanns-whatscook-222122); [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/hellmanns-successfully-taps-whatsapp/en-gb/33683); [The One Show](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/theoneshow/-network/FLAG%20[s]%20Interpublic/2015)
- Gap: no Cannes medal could be confirmed from the search results.

**Case 15: "Eva.Stories" (Leo Burnett Israel), May 2019. Story told through a fictional character's Instagram Stories.**
- 220 Instagram Stories videos imagined what a real Holocaust diarist, Eva Heyman, would have posted if she had had a smartphone in 1944. — [Direct New Ideas](https://directnewideas.com/magazine/leo_burnett_israel_eva_stories_/?id=2839); [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/eva-stories-project-brings-a-real-life-holocaust-diary-to-instagram)
- Claimed results: over 1M followers in 24 hours and 1.7M in total; 330M+ Story views in 89 countries; about 1M views per video. Two One Show Gold Pencils and Best of Discipline. — [The One Club](https://www.oneclub.org/articles/-view/eva-stories); [Direct New Ideas](https://directnewideas.com/magazine/leo_burnett_israel_eva_stories_/?id=2839)

**Case 16: ABC *Lost*, "The Lost Experience" (2006) and "Find 815" (2007-08). In-world websites.**
- Clues lived on the fictional Hanso Foundation website. Players revealed them by clicking faintly marked anomalies in the page design or by entering codes. ABC's marketing SVP called it a hybrid between content and marketing. Find 815 started with a phone number and a website for the fictional Oceanic airline. — [Lost Experience (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Experience); [Find 815 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Find_815)

**Case 17: Cherry Coke "The Lost Island of Alanna", 1998. An early beverage-brand ARG.**
- Clues were spread across the Coca-Cola brand's website and a fictional university site that posted journal entries as clues. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Island_of_Alanna); [List of ARGs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_alternate_reality_games)

### Inferences
- Rewards for being first plus scarcity drive urgency (Decoded; Hotel 626's 6pm-6am window). Promo codes hidden in a brand's own ads and posts make the paid media itself the game board (McDonald's SG). For a delivery or menu app, an in-app redemption code at the end of each clue chain ties the game directly to orders.
- Community solving on Reddit, forums and Discord multiplies reach (Why So Serious, Stranger Things), but players will punish a payoff that feels like a sales pitch (the Stranger Things NFT reveal).
- Messaging apps (WhatsApp, Instagram Stories, Tinder) feel personal and native. Eva.Stories and WhatsCook show this works for storytelling and utility, which suits MENA markets where WhatsApp use is heavy (that last point is inference; I gathered no MENA usage data here).

### Gaps
- *I Love Bees* (Halo 2, 2004), *Year Zero* (NIN), Cicada 3301, Cloverfield's "Slusho!" and Wendy's "Feast of Legends" are widely cited, but I gathered no sourced figures for them in this session, so they are not included as cases.
- No sourced Deliveroo, Uber Eats, Talabat, Careem, Domino's or Burger King clue or ARG campaign was found. My searches returned only coupon aggregators, plus Burger King app puzzles (e.g., the 2024 Impossible Whopper daily word puzzle for 50 Crowns, from [Hunt4Freebies](https://www.hunt4freebies.com/burger-king-impossible-whopper-puzzle-50-free-crowns-per-day-through-8-11-24/)).

## Q3. What results and lessons, including failures, are documented?

### Takeaway
Documented wins share four traits: a narrative that fits the brand, an interface native to the platform (phone UI, Instagram, WhatsApp, AR), a clear reward or payoff, and a design that invites shared solving. Documented failures and risks are deception backlash (Ex Machina), a disappointing commercial reveal (Stranger Things NFT), and self-reported metrics that are hard to verify.

### Cited Findings
- Scale benchmarks: Why So Serious had 10-11M participants in 75+ countries ([42 Entertainment](https://42entertainment.com/work/whysoserious)). Hotel 626 had 4M+ visitors with no media spend ([Cannes Lions](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/doritos-290056)). Lost Ring had 2M+ users ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/89387/akqa-mcdonalds.html)). Scoop Snoop had 500K+ players and about 4B impressions ([Cannes Lions](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/scoops-ahoy-operation-scoop-snoop-724663)). Eva.Stories had 1.7M followers and 330M views ([The One Club](https://www.oneclub.org/articles/-view/eva-stories)).
- Awards benchmarks: Why So Serious won the Cyber Grand Prix in 2009 ([ClickZ](https://clickz.com/?p=67833)). Decoded won the Outdoor and Integrated Grand Prix in 2011 ([Adland](https://www.adland.tv/cannes-integrated-grand-prix-and-titanium-2011)). Hotel 626 won a D&AD Yellow Pencil ([D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/work/d-ad-awards-archive/hotel-626)).
- Failure or controversy, deception: Ex Machina's Tinder stunt was criticized as catfishing and for lacking a moment that lets the "victim" in on the joke. It still lifted consumption 353% ([ClickZ](https://clickz.com/?p=26814); [Time](https://time.com/3746083/tinder-sxsw-catfishing-ex-machina)).
- Failure or controversy, payoff mismatch: Reddit solvers of the Stranger Things 4 trail "weren't exactly kind" when it turned out to be an NFT launch ([What's on Netflix](https://whats-on-netflix.com/news/stranger-things-nfts-to-launch-ahead-of-season-4)).
- Regulatory risk: Hotel 626 and other Frito-Lay immersive campaigns were cited in a consumer-advocacy (CDD) complaint about marketing to young people ([CDD appendix](https://www.foodpolitics.com/wp-content/uploads/Final-Appendices-to-CDD-Complaint.pdf)).
- Measurement caveat: most figures above come from agency award entries and are not independently audited (stated by the sources as self-reported entry claims).

### Inferences
- For a menu or food-ordering app (aimenu), the evidence points to this design: a mobile web "lost phone" of a fictional food lover or chef. Clues are spread across chats, photos and notes. Restaurant or dish names act as passwords. Each chain resolves into an in-app promo code (McDonald's SG model). Real-world extensions could include a WhatsApp number or Instagram account for the character (WhatsCook / Eva.Stories / Ex Machina model), a time-limited window, and first-solver prizes (Hotel 626 / Decoded). A Discord or X hashtag would support community solving.
- Avoid real deception, such as fake personas that are never revealed or mimicking a real person's phone. Make the payoff feel like a reward, not a bait-and-switch. Disclose the brand clearly at the end, and consider privacy and age-appropriateness.

### Gaps
- No documented ROI or sales data was found for most cases. Doritos' bag-sales figure comes from a third-party complaint exhibit, and the Baskin-Robbins share data is from the Australian campaign, not the AR game.
- No MENA (Saudi/GCC) examples of this format were found in this research pass. They should be researched separately, including Arabic-language sources.
